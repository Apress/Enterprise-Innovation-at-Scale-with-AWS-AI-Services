# State Pay Requirements by Jurisdiction

## Overview

Pay transparency laws are expanding across the United States. This guide covers current requirements for major jurisdictions where Supertron LLC may post positions. Compliance requirements vary by location.

---

## CALIFORNIA

### Law
California Senate Bill 1162 (Labor Code § 244)

### Effective Date
January 1, 2024

### Who It Applies To
- Private employers posting positions available to California residents
- Even if position is not physically in California
- Includes remote positions

### Requirement
**Employers must include a pay range in any job posting.**

### What Must Be Included
- Minimum compensation (specific number)
- Maximum compensation (specific number)
- Can be salary, wages, commission, bonus, or combination
- Must be in numbers, not "competitive" or vague terms

### Format Requirements
```
COMPLIANT:
Salary: $85,000 - $120,000 per year

COMPLIANT:
Compensation: $4,000 - $6,500 per month

NOT COMPLIANT:
Competitive salary
```

### Regional Pay Bands
If serving multiple California regions, must either:
- Provide region-specific ranges, OR
- Provide one range covering all regions

### Exceptions
- Position filled entirely from internal promotion (if not advertised externally)
- Employer could not ascertain range in good faith (rare; requires documentation)

### Penalty
- $100-$10,000 per violation (per job posting)
- Private right of action
- Attorney's fees may be awarded

### Best Practice
Determine your range first, based on:
- Market research
- Budget constraints
- Experience levels you're considering
- Regional differences if applicable

## COLORADO

### Law
Colorado Equal Pay Act (CRS 24-34-401) - Amended to include transparency

### Effective Date
January 1, 2021

### Who It Applies To
- Employers with employees in Colorado
- Positions advertised in Colorado or to Colorado residents
- Includes remote positions available to Colorado applicants

### Requirement
**Employers must include a pay range when advertising a position.**

### What Must Be Included
- Pay range for the position
- Based on actual expectation for hire
- Cannot be inflated or arbitrary

### Format Requirements
```
COMPLIANT:
Salary: $70,000 - $100,000 per year

COMPLIANT:
Hourly: $40 - $55 per hour
```

### Special Considerations
- Pay range must be reasonable and defensible
- Cannot be excessively broad (though law doesn't specify exact limits)
- Employers cannot screen out applicants based on salary history

### Exceptions
- Extremely limited; similar to California

### Penalty
- Not specified in statute
- Enforcement through complaint mechanism

### Best Practice
Follow similar practices to California. Colorado is slightly less prescriptive but expects reasonable, defensible ranges.

## NEW YORK

### Law
New York City Local Law 1296 (effective 2023); State SB-3249 (effective 2024)

### Effective Dates
- NYC: January 1, 2023
- Statewide: September 2024

### Who It Applies To
- NYC: Employers with 4+ employees posting positions in NYC or to NYC residents
- State: Employers with 4+ employees statewide

### Requirement
**Employers must include a pay range when advertising any position.**

### What Must Be Included
- Minimum and maximum salary/compensation
- Must be realistic for the position
- Based on role, not applicant credentials

### Format Requirements
```
COMPLIANT:
Annual Salary: $65,000 - $95,000

COMPLIANT:
Hourly Rate: $35 - $50 per hour
```

### Additional Requirements
- For internal job postings: Must also disclose pay range to current employees
- Cannot ask candidates for salary history

### Exceptions
- Positions filled from internal promotion pool only (if not advertised externally)

### Penalty
- NYC: Up to $5,000 per violation
- Statewide: Enforced through penalty mechanism

### Best Practice
Ensure ranges are specific and defensible. NYC is more prescriptive than Colorado; follow California's approach.

## WASHINGTON

### Who It Applies To
- Employers with 15+ employees
- Positions advertised in Washington or to Washington residents

### Requirement
**Employers must provide pay range when listing a job opening.**

### What Must Be Included
- Pay range for the position
- Based on actual compensation expectation
- Specific numbers required

### Format Requirements
```
COMPLIANT:
Salary: $75,000 - $110,000 per year

COMPLIANT:
Hourly: $45 - $65 per hour
```

### Special Considerations
- Covers salary and other forms of compensation
- Cannot request prior salary history from applicants
- Ranges must be reasonable and defensible

### Exceptions
- Positions filled exclusively from internal promotion

### Penalty
- Enforced through complaint mechanism
- Amounts not specified in statute

### Best Practice
Follow California/NYC standards. Washington is less strict than CA but still requires clear ranges.

## MULTI-STATE BEST PRACTICES

If Supertron LLC posts in multiple states:

### Option 1: Single Range
Use highest minimum and highest maximum across all locations:
```
SALARY: $80,000 - $130,000 per year
(Adjusted based on location and experience)
```

### Option 2: Region-Specific Ranges
Specify for each major market:
```
San Francisco Bay Area: $110,000 - $150,000
Los Angeles: $95,000 - $130,000
Denver: $90,000 - $125,000
New York City: $100,000 - $140,000
```

### Option 3: Ranges by Cost of Living
Group regions by similar costs:
```
High Cost of Living (CA, NY): $110,000 - $150,000
Medium Cost of Living (CO, WA): $90,000 - $125,000
Remote (Lower cost regions): $75,000 - $110,000
```

### Recommendation
Use Option 2 or 3 for transparency and to attract diverse talent across regions.

## PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION

### Step 1: Determine Your Range
For each position, establish:
- Minimum you'll pay for a qualified entry candidate
- Maximum you'll pay for an expert in the role
- Base on market research and budget

### Step 2: Apply State Rules
- California or posting to CA? Include range (required)
- Colorado, NY, WA, or other listed states? Include range (recommended or required)
- Other states? Include range (best practice even if not required)

### Step 3: Document Your Decision
Keep records of:
- How range was determined
- Market research consulted
- Date range was set
- Regional adjustments (if any)

### Step 4: Train Hiring Managers
Ensure hiring managers know:
- The posted range is what you're willing to pay
- They should hire within the range
- They can't promise additional compensation outside range

### Step 5: Monitor Changes
- If regulations change, update your process
- Review ranges quarterly against market data
- Update future postings as needed

## FAQ

**Q: Can we adjust the posted range once posted?**
A: Once posted, the range is what you commit to. If adjusting for new hire, document the business reason.

**Q: What if we don't know our budget yet?**
A: Determine budget before posting. Don't post without a range.

**Q: Can we post a wide range to be flexible?**
A: Legally yes, but practically no. Ranges should be realistic (generally within 50% spread). Overly broad ranges damage credibility.

**Q: Do we have to pay at minimum of range?**
A: No. The range is what you're willing to pay. Hire at any point in the range.

**Q: What if someone negotiates above the range?**
A: Typically you don't pay above the posted range, as it was your stated maximum. If you do, document the business justification.

**Q: Remote positions - which state rules apply?**
A: All states where the position is available to residents. If open to CA, CO, NY residents, all three apply.
