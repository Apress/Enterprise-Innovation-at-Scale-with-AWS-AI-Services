# Supertron LLC Corporate Hiring Standards Handbook

## Section 1: Introduction and Purpose

This handbook outlines Supertron LLC's commitment to inclusive, equitable hiring practices. All job postings must align with these standards before publication.

**Our Mission:** Attract diverse talent, remove barriers to application, and ensure all candidates feel welcome.

**Scope:** This handbook applies to all job postings by Supertron LLC, including permanent positions, contract roles, and temporary assignments.

---

## Section 2: Non-Discrimination Policy

### Protected Classes

Supertron LLC does not discriminate based on:
- Age (actual or perceived)
- Race or color
- Gender or gender identity
- Sexual orientation
- National origin
- Disability (including reasonable accommodations)
- Veteran status
- Religion
- Genetic information

This applies to all aspects of employment, including recruitment, hiring, promotion, and compensation.

### Job Posting Principles

Every job posting must:
1. Focus on job-related qualifications
2. Avoid coded language that excludes protected groups
3. Include reasonable accommodation language
4. Be welcoming to diverse candidates
5. Provide transparent compensation

## Section 3: Language Standards

### 3.1 Inclusive Language Requirements

**Requirement:** Use gender-neutral language in all postings.

**Instead of:**
- "He/she will manage..."
- "Salesmen needed"
- "Girl Friday"
- "Customer service girls"

**Use:**
- "The successful candidate will..."
- "Sales professionals needed"
- "Administrative professional"
- "Customer service specialists"

### 3.2 Age-Appropriate Language

**Requirement:** Avoid coded language that appeals only to younger candidates or assumes age.

**Language to Avoid:**
- "Digital native" (implies youth)
- "Young, energetic team" (age proxy)
- "Comfortable with all-nighters" (discriminatory physical requirement)
- "Tech-savvy millennial" (age-specific)
- "Fast-paced, startup culture" (code for no work-life balance; attracts younger workers)
- "Rockstar," "Ninja," "Guru" (youth-coded industry jargon)
- "Recent graduate preferred" (clear age discrimination)

**Acceptable Alternatives:**
- "Proficient with modern development tools"
- "Motivated professional"
- "Able to manage rapid development cycles"
- "Skilled technical specialist"
- "Collaborative team environment"
- "Expert-level skills in [specific technology]"
- "Strong foundation in [technical area]"

### 3.3 Avoiding Ableist Language

**Requirement:** Do not use terms that stigmatize disability or health conditions.

**Language to Avoid:**
- "Crazy," "Insane," "Lame," "Stupid," "Dumb"
- "Wheelchair accessible only if needed" (implies disability is unusual)
- "Normal candidates only"
- "As long as you don't have [disability]"
- "We're a mental-health-friendly company" (implies others aren't)

**Acceptable Approaches:**
- "Accessible facilities including wheelchair access, accessible parking, accessible restrooms"
- "We provide reasonable accommodations for qualified individuals with disabilities"
- "Flexible work arrangements available"
- "Supportive workplace for all abilities"

### 3.4 Avoiding Gendered or Stereotypical Language

**Language to Avoid:**
- "Requires taking direction well" (stereotypically applied to women)
- "Aggressive negotiator" (stereotypically applied to men)
- "Mother of the office" (gender-stereotypical role)
- "Needs to be a team player" (vague; often code for conformity)
- References to appearance, attractiveness, or physical characteristics unrelated to the job

**Better Approaches:**
- "Communicates clearly and persuasively"
- "Experienced negotiator"
- "Collaborates effectively with colleagues"
- Focus on specific job competencies, not personality archetypes

### 3.5 Avoiding Socioeconomic Discrimination

**Language to Avoid:**
- "We only hire Ivy League graduates"
- "Requires perfect credit score" (unrelated to job performance)
- "Must own a car" (excludes public transit users; may exclude lower-income candidates)
- "Unpaid internship prerequisites" (excludes those who can't afford to work for free)

**Better Approaches:**
- "Bachelor's degree in [field] or equivalent professional experience"
- "Valid driver's license required only if driving is an essential job function"
- "Paid position with competitive compensation"
- "Internship must be compensated"

## Section 4: Qualification Standards

### 4.1 Essential vs. Preferred Qualifications

**Essential Qualifications:** Must-haves for the role
- Skills directly required to perform core job functions
- Certifications or licenses legally required
- Years of specific, relevant experience (if truly necessary)

**Preferred Qualifications:** Nice-to-haves
- Additional skills that enhance performance but aren't required
- Industry knowledge that can be learned on the job
- Soft skills that can be developed

**Why This Matters:** Strict essential requirements filter out capable candidates, reducing diversity. Preferred qualifications invite applications from candidates with potential.

### 4.2 Years of Experience Guidance

**Problem with Years Requirements:**
Research shows years of experience are a proxy for age. Using overly specific years can exclude older workers or younger workers who achieved skills through alternate paths.

**How to Frame Experience:**

Instead of: "10+ years required"

Try: "Significant experience with [specific technology/domain], typically 8+ years but equivalent expertise through alternative paths considered"

Or better: Focus on skills, not years:
"Demonstrated expertise in Python, AWS architecture, and microservices design"

### 4.3 Education Requirements

**Requirement:** Education standards must be job-related.

**Avoid:**
- "Bachelor's degree preferred" (when not truly needed)
- "Top-tier school graduates only"
- "MBA required" (unless directly relevant)

**Better:**
- "Bachelor's degree in Computer Science or equivalent professional experience"
- "Demonstrated knowledge of [subject]" (regardless of how acquired)
- "5+ years relevant experience may substitute for degree requirement"

### 4.4 Special Circumstances

**Remote Work Eligibility:**
Clearly state if position is:
- Fully remote (anywhere US/worldwide)
- Remote but requires office presence X days/month
- Hybrid (specify locations and days)
- Office-required (specify location)

**Travel Requirements:**
If travel is needed, specify:
- Frequency ("Occasional" vs. "50% of time")
- Type ("Mostly regional" vs. "International")

**Physical Requirements:**
Only list if truly essential and job-related:
- Instead of: "Must be able to lift 50 lbs"
- Better: "Position involves lifting up to 50 lbs regularly; reasonable accommodations available"

## Section 5: Compensation and Benefits Disclosure

### 5.1 Pay Range Disclosure (Required)

**Requirement:** All job postings must include a pay range.

**Format:**
```
Salary: $[Minimum] - $[Maximum] per [year/month]
```

**Explanation permitted but not required:**
```
Salary: $85,000 - $115,000 per year, based on experience and qualifications
```

### 5.2 What to Include in Compensation Discussion

If you mention benefits, include:
- Health insurance (type/coverage)
- 401(k) match percentage (if offered)
- Paid time off (PTO days/weeks)
- Professional development budget
- Stock options or equity (if offered)
- Bonus structure (if standard)
- Flexible work arrangements

**Why:** Candidates can make informed decisions. Transparency builds trust.

### 5.3 What NOT to Promise

Avoid:
- "Unlimited PTO" (usually means unclear expectations; rarely generous)
- "As much as we can afford" (vague; sounds desperate)
- "Huge signing bonus" (be specific)
- Promising benefits not yet approved by finance/HR

## Section 6: Reasonable Accommodations and Accessibility

### 6.1 Required Accommodation Language

**All job postings must include:**

```
Supertron LLC is an Equal Opportunity Employer. We provide reasonable
accommodations for qualified individuals with disabilities. If you need
a reasonable accommodation to apply or interview, please contact [HR Contact]
at [email] or [phone].
```

### 6.2 What "Reasonable Accommodation" Means

Examples of common accommodations:
- Modified work schedule or remote work
- Accessible office space (wheelchair ramp, accessible parking, accessible restrooms)
- Assistive technology (screen reader, speech-to-text, large monitor)
- Modified job duties (essential functions remain; peripheral tasks adapted)
- Leave for medical treatment
- Job coaching or mentoring

### 6.3 Accessibility of Job Postings

**Requirement:** Job postings must be accessible to candidates with disabilities.

**How to Ensure:**
- Use clear, readable fonts (not tiny text or unusual colors)
- Include alt text for any images or icons
- Structure with clear headings (helps screen readers)
- Avoid long walls of unbroken text
- Don't use color alone to convey information
- Provide contact info for accessibility questions

## Section 7: Essential Functions Definition

### 7.1 Defining Essential Functions

**Essential functions** are job duties that are:
- Core to the role
- Could not be reasonably eliminated
- Directly related to the job objective

Under the ADA, you must be able to justify that a function is truly essential.

**How to Present:**

Include a section like:
```
Essential Functions:
- Analyze customer data and generate reports (40% of time)
- Present findings to leadership (20% of time)
- Collaborate with product team (30% of time)
- Attend monthly all-hands meetings (10% of time)
```

### 7.2 What NOT to Include as Essential

These are usually preferences or cultural fit, not essential:
- "Must be a team player" (vague; hard to justify legally)
- "Willing to work weekends" (unless truly needed)
- "Must socialize after hours" (job requirement?)
- "Must fit our culture" (can enable discrimination)

### 7.3 Reasonable Accommodations for Essential Functions

If something is truly essential, explain what support you'd provide:

**Instead of:** "Must be able to attend all in-person meetings"

**Better:** "Position requires collaboration with in-office team; can participate via video conference from alternate location with prior arrangement"

## Section 9: Review Process

### 9.1 Job Posting Review Checklist

Before publishing any job posting:

**Content Check:**
- [ ] No prohibited or age-biased language
- [ ] Gender-neutral throughout
- [ ] Inclusive and accessible language
- [ ] No ableist terms
- [ ] Essential vs. preferred qualifications clearly distinguished
- [ ] Reasonable accommodation language included
- [ ] Compensation range disclosed (SB 1162 compliant)
- [ ] Remote/hybrid/office status clear
- [ ] Hiring manager aware of standards

**Accessibility Check:**
- [ ] Font is readable (11pt or larger)
- [ ] Sufficient contrast between text and background
- [ ] Organized with clear headings
- [ ] No walls of unbroken text
- [ ] Contact info for accessibility accommodations included

**Compliance Check:**
- [ ] Posting doesn't require any illegal qualifications
- [ ] Posting doesn't imply discrimination
- [ ] All regulations (SB 1162, ADA, EEOC) met

### 9.2 Escalation

If any of the above checks fail, route to HR Legal for revision before posting.

## Appendix: Examples

### Example 1: POORLY Written Job Posting

```
Hiring rockstar software engineer! Young, energetic developers only.
Must be comfortable with all-nighters and fast-paced startup culture.
Digital natives preferred. Recent grad or Ivy League background a plus.
Compensation depends on qualifications.

Required: 10+ years experience with latest tech stack. Must own car for
client visits. Must fit our family-like culture.
```

**Issues:**
- Age-biased language: "Young," "energetic," "digital native," "recent grad"
- Jargon: "Rockstar"
- Socioeconomic: "Must own car," no compensation
- Vague: "Depends on qualifications," "latest tech stack"
- Missing: Reasonable accommodations, SB 1162 range
- Ableist: "All-nighters"

### Example 2: COMPLIANT Job Posting

```
Software Engineer - Full Stack

About the Role:
We're seeking an experienced Software Engineer to join our platform team.
You'll build and maintain microservices, collaborate with product teams,
and help scale our infrastructure.

Location: San Francisco (hybrid; 2 days/week in-office)

Essential Functions:
- Design and implement backend APIs (40% of time)
- Collaborate with frontend engineers and product teams (30% of time)
- Participate in code review and testing (20% of time)
- Attend team meetings and planning sessions (10% of time)

Qualifications:
**Required:**
- Demonstrated expertise in Python and relational databases
- Experience building scalable systems (typically 5+ years, but equivalent
  skills through alternative paths considered)
- Strong problem-solving and communication abilities

**Preferred:**
- Experience with AWS or similar cloud platforms
- Background in microservices architecture
- Familiarity with Agile development practices

Compensation & Benefits:
- Salary: $120,000 - $160,000 per year, based on experience and skills
- Comprehensive health insurance (medical, dental, vision)
- 401(k) with 4% employer match
- 20 days PTO + 10 company holidays
- $1,500 annual professional development budget
- Flexible work arrangements

Accessibility:
Supertron LLC is an Equal Opportunity Employer. We are committed to
building a diverse team and provide reasonable accommodations for qualified
individuals with disabilities. If you need an accommodation to apply or
interview, please contact our HR team at careers@supertron.com.

```

**Why This Works:**
- Clear role and expectations
- No age, gender, or ableist language
- Transparent compensation (SB 1162 compliant)
- Skills-focused, not years-of-experience focused
- Explicit accommodation language
- Accessibility-friendly formatting
- Professional and inclusive tone
