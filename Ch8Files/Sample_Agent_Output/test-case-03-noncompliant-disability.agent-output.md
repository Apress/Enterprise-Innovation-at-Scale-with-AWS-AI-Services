## 2. Violations

### 🔴 HIGH Severity

---

#### Violation 1 — ADA: Explicit Exclusion of People with Disabilities
**Regulation:** Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), Title I

**Problematic language:**
- *"Perfect physical health — no disabilities or health issues"*
- *"Cannot have any physical limitations"*
- *"No deaf or hard of hearing candidates (we use verbal communication only, no accommodations)"*
- *"No mental health issues or history of mental illness"*
- *"Must be 'normal' and mentally stable"*

**Why it's a problem:** The ADA prohibits employers from excluding qualified individuals based on disability. These statements do exactly that — explicitly screening out candidates with physical or mental health conditions. The phrase *"no accommodations"* for deaf/hard-of-hearing candidates directly violates the ADA's reasonable accommodation mandate. Employers must assess accommodation requests individually; they cannot issue a blanket refusal in a job posting. The phrase *"normal"* is also prohibited as it implies people with disabilities are abnormal.

**Fix:** Remove all of the above language entirely. Replace with a description of legitimate, essential physical functions (e.g., *"Must be able to navigate a standard office environment; reasonable accommodations available for qualified individuals"*) and add a formal accommodations statement.








#### Violation 2 — Title VII (Gender): "Office Mother Figure" and Gender Stereotyping
**Regulation:** Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964; EEOC Gender Discrimination Guidelines

**Problematic language:**
- *"The perfect office mother figure"*

**Why it's a problem:** Describing a role using a gendered, female-coded archetype ("mother figure") constitutes gender discrimination under Title VII. This language signals that women are preferred candidates, excludes men and non-binary individuals, and reinforces harmful workplace stereotypes. Language such as "Girl Friday" and "mother of the office" is explicitly flagged in compliance guidance as prohibited.

**Fix:** Replace with a neutral, competency-based description: *"A highly organized, proactive administrative professional..."*








#### Violation 3 — ADA: No Accommodations Statement
**Regulation:** ADA Title I; EEOC Posting Requirements

**Why it's a problem:** Every job posting must include language inviting candidates to request reasonable accommodations for the application and interview process. This posting contains no such statement — and actively states the opposite ("no accommodations"). A legally compliant accommodations statement is a required element, not optional.

**Fix:** Add: *"If you require a reasonable accommodation to apply or interview for this position, please contact [HR contact] at [email/phone]."*








#### Violation 4 — ADEA: Discriminatory Experience Requirement
**Regulation:** Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), 29 U.S.C. § 623

**Problematic language:**
- *"At least 20+ years of executive assistant experience (no alternatives)"*

**Why it's a problem:** Requiring 20+ years of experience with no alternatives serves as a de facto age proxy. Someone with 20+ years in a specific field would typically be 40 years old or older at minimum — meaning this requirement functions as an age filter. While experience requirements are not automatically illegal, extreme minimums — particularly when combined with "no alternatives" — are directly scrutinized under the ADEA. Compliance guidance already flags "10+ years required" as suspect; 20+ years with no alternative is a much stronger red flag.

**Fix:** Replace with a competency-based requirement: *"Extensive experience supporting C-level executives, with demonstrated mastery of [specific skills]."*








#### Violation 5 — FLSA: Explicit Statement of Uncompensated Overtime
**Regulation:** Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA); California Labor Code

**Problematic language:**
- *"Overtime not compensated"*
- *"Monday–Friday, 8am–6pm minimum" + "Weekend work required"*

**Why it's a problem:** At a stated salary of $45,000–$55,000, this role may not qualify for FLSA's administrative or executive exemptions. If the role is non-exempt, all hours over 40/week must be compensated at 1.5x the regular rate under the FLSA — and California law requires daily overtime after 8 hours. Explicitly stating "overtime not compensated" in a posting for a likely non-exempt role is a significant wage-and-hour violation. Compliance guidance states that weekend or extended work expectations must be described as "compensated accordingly."

**Fix:** Remove the "overtime not compensated" statement. Consult legal counsel to properly classify this role as exempt or non-exempt, and describe scheduling expectations accurately and lawfully.








#### Violation 6 — Self-Contradicting EEO Statement
**Regulation:** EEOC; Title VII; ADA

**Problematic language:**
- *"Supertron is an equal opportunity employer in all respects except as stated in this job posting."*

**Why it's a problem:** An EEO statement that explicitly carves out exceptions for the content of the same posting is not a legally valid EEO statement — it is an acknowledgment that the posting discriminates. A valid EEO statement must apply unconditionally across all protected classes. This language could be used as evidence of intentional discrimination in any future enforcement action or litigation.

**Fix:** Replace entirely with a standard, unconditional EEO statement covering all protected classes (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, genetic information, sexual orientation, gender identity).

### 🟠 MEDIUM Severity

---

#### Violation 7 — Ableist Language Throughout
**Regulation:** ADA; EEOC Language Guidelines

**Problematic language:**
- *"insane role," "crazy deadlines," "multitask without going crazy," "lame," "dumb"*

**Why it's a problem:** Terms like "crazy," "insane," "lame," and "dumb" are classified as ableist language — they use disability and mental health conditions as casual pejoratives. Compliance guidance explicitly requires these terms to be removed from all job postings. Beyond the legal exposure, this language signals an unwelcoming culture for candidates with disabilities or mental health conditions.

**Fix:** Replace with professional, descriptive alternatives: *"fast-paced," "deadline-driven," "high-volume," "complex."*








#### Violation 8 — ADA / California FEHA: "Never Taken a Sick Day" as a Preferred Criterion

**Problematic language:**
- *"Preferred: Never taken a sick day"*

**Why it's a problem:** Screening for candidates who have never used sick leave is a proxy for screening out people with chronic illness or disability — both protected under the ADA and California's Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA). This criterion also conflicts with California's legally protected sick leave entitlements under the Healthy Workplaces, Healthy Families Act.

**Fix:** Remove entirely.

---

#### Violation 9 — California FEHA / Title VII: "No Family Responsibilities or Dependents"

**Problematic language:**
- *"Preferred: No family responsibilities or dependents"*

**Why it's a problem:** Under California's FEHA, familial status is a protected characteristic. This criterion disproportionately impacts women (pregnancy, parental leave) and caregivers. Even as a "preferred" criterion, it signals discriminatory intent and creates legal exposure.

**Fix:** Remove entirely.








#### Violation 10 — Credit Score Screening for a Non-Financial Role
**Regulation:** California ICRAA; EEOC Guidance on Background Checks

**Problematic language:**
- *"Perfect credit score (we verify this as part of background check)"*

**Why it's a problem:** Credit checks for positions that do not involve meaningful financial oversight disproportionately affect protected groups (racial minorities, lower-income candidates) and are disfavored under EEOC guidance. Compliance guidance explicitly flags "perfect credit score required" as discriminatory for non-financial roles. An executive assistant role as described does not appear to meet the threshold to justify credit screening.

**Fix:** Remove the credit score requirement. If a background check is conducted, ensure it is job-related, consistently applied, and compliant with California's ICRAA notice requirements.








#### Violation 11 — Socioeconomic Discrimination: "Unpaid Internship Background"
**Regulation:** FLSA; EEOC Socioeconomic Discrimination Guidance

**Problematic language:**
- *"Unpaid internship background showing willingness to work for free"*

**Why it's a problem:** Valuing unpaid internship experience excludes candidates from lower socioeconomic backgrounds who could not afford to work without compensation. Compliance guidance explicitly prohibits "unpaid internship" as a screening criterion because it functions as a socioeconomic filter.

**Fix:** Remove entirely, or reframe as: *"Internship or entry-level administrative experience."*








#### Violation 12 — ADA: Phone-Only Interviews Exclude Candidates with Disabilities
**Regulation:** ADA — Reasonable Accommodation in the Hiring Process

**Problematic language:**
- *"Phone interviews only (sorry, no video options available)"*

**Why it's a problem:** Restricting interviews to phone-only format can exclude deaf or hard-of-hearing candidates, or those who rely on captioning or ASL interpretation. The ADA requires that the hiring *process itself* be accessible. This is especially problematic given the posting's stated "no accommodations" position elsewhere.

**Fix:** Offer multiple interview formats: *"Interviews conducted by phone or video; please contact us to discuss accessibility accommodations."*

### 🟡 LOW Severity (Flag for Review)

---

#### Violation 13 — SB 1162: Benefits Listed as "TBD"
**Regulation:** California SB 1162 (Labor Code § 244); Best Practice

**Why it's a problem:** While the salary range of $45,000–$55,000 includes specific numbers (meeting the letter of SB 1162), listing benefits as "TBD based on budget" is a red flag. Candidates in California are entitled to understand their total compensation. Benefits must be defined before posting.

**Fix:** Define benefits clearly: *"Full benefits package including [health, dental, vision, PTO] — details available upon request."*








#### Violation 14 — ADA: Accessibility Statement Is Inadvertently Othering

**Problematic language:**
- *"Wheelchair access available if needed, but we've never had someone in a wheelchair work here so this is a new situation for us."*

**Why it's a problem:** While mentioning accessibility is positive, characterizing wheelchair users as an unusual novelty signals that people with disabilities may not be genuinely welcome. Compliance guidance specifically flags this exact framing as problematic.

**Fix:** Replace with: *"Our facilities are fully accessible, including wheelchair access, accessible parking, and accessible restrooms."*
