## 2. Violations

### 🔴 HIGH SEVERITY — Clear Legal Violations

---

#### **H-1: Missing Pay Range (California SB 1162)**
- **Issue:** The posting states compensation is "negotiable," "flexible," and "depends on what we can afford" — all of which are explicitly non-compliant formulations. No minimum or maximum salary figure is provided.
- **Regulation:** California SB 1162 (Labor Code § 244), effective January 1, 2024. Applies to all positions posted in California or available to California residents.
- **Penalty:** $100–$10,000 per violation per job posting; private right of action; attorney's fees may be awarded.
- **Fix:** Replace the compensation section with a specific numerical range, e.g., *"Salary: $150,000 – $210,000 per year."* Vague language like "competitive" or "TBD" is also non-compliant.

---

#### **H-2: Age Discrimination — Pervasive Proxy Language (ADEA)**
- **Issue:** The posting is saturated with language that courts and the EEOC treat as proxies for age discrimination under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), which protects workers 40 and older. The following terms appear and are each individually problematic:

| Prohibited Term | Why It's a Problem |
|---|---|
| *"Young and energetic team"* | Direct age implication |
| *"Digital native"* | Generational/youth proxy |
| *"Startup culture"* | Youth-coded; implies age preference |
| *"Rockstar," "ninja-level"* | Youth-coded industry jargon |
| *"Tech-savvy millennial"* | Explicitly age/generation-coded |
| *"Recent graduate preferred"* | Clear age discrimination |
| *"Comfortable with all-nighters"* | Excludes older workers; ableist overlap |
| *"Stay current with tech trends"* | Implies older workers fall behind |

- **Regulation:** Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), 29 U.S.C. § 621 et seq.; California FEPA.
- **Fix:** Replace each term per the alternatives table in Section 5 below.

---

#### **H-3: Contradictory Qualifications — Legal Risk and Internal Inconsistency**
- **Issue:** The posting simultaneously requires *"15+ years of software development experience"* (a strong age proxy when combined with the rest of the language) AND lists *"recent graduate with startup experience"* as a preferred qualification. These are mutually exclusive and the combination strongly signals age-based screening in both directions.
- **Additional concern:** Requiring 15+ years with no flexibility or skills-based alternative may be challenged as an age proxy. Research and company guidance indicate that rigid experience thresholds can have disparate impact on protected age groups.
- **Regulation:** ADEA; EEOC disparate impact doctrine.
- **Fix:** Replace with skills-based language: *"Demonstrated expertise in [specific technologies], typically acquired over 8+ years, though equivalent expertise through alternative paths will be considered."*

---

#### **H-4: Defective EEO Statement**
- **Issue:** *"Supertron LLC is an equal opportunity employer… we think"* is not a valid EEO statement. The hedging language (*"we think"*) could be read as an admission that the company does not actually commit to equal opportunity, which is both legally and reputationally damaging.
- **Regulation:** EEOC requirements; California FEPA.
- **Fix:** Replace with a complete, unambiguous EEO statement. See Section 5 for a compliant example.

---

### 🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY — Significant Concerns Requiring Revision

---

#### **M-1: Ableist Language (ADA)**
- **Issue:** The following language violates ADA best practices and may constitute disability discrimination by implying that applicants with health conditions, disabilities, or physical limitations are unwelcome:
  - *"Comfortable with all-nighters"* / *"60+ hour weeks"* — excludes candidates with chronic illness, disabilities, or caregiving responsibilities.
  - *"Not for the faint of heart"* — stigmatizes candidates with cardiac conditions or mental health considerations.
  - *"High-pressure environment"* without accommodation language — potentially discouraging to candidates with anxiety or stress-related conditions.
- **Regulation:** Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), 42 U.S.C. § 12101; California FEHA.
- **Fix:** Replace with business-neutral language (e.g., *"deadline-driven environment with occasional extended hours"*) and add a reasonable accommodations statement.

---

#### **M-2: Missing Reasonable Accommodations Statement (ADA)**
- **Issue:** No accommodation language is present anywhere in the posting. This is a required element for ADA compliance and must include contact information for accommodation requests.
- **Regulation:** ADA, 42 U.S.C. § 12112.
- **Fix:** Add language such as: *"If you require a reasonable accommodation to apply for or perform this role, please contact [HR email/phone]."*

---

#### **M-3: Vague and Non-Specific Job Requirements**
- **Issue:** Responsibilities such as *"Write code"* and *"Stay current with tech trends"* are insufficient to distinguish essential from preferred functions — a requirement under ADA. Vague requirements also make it impossible to assess whether qualifications are job-related, which is required under Title VII disparate impact doctrine.
- **Fix:** Define specific, measurable responsibilities and distinguish clearly between essential functions and preferred qualifications.

---

#### **M-4: "Willing to Relocate Immediately" — Potential Disparate Impact**
- **Issue:** Requiring immediate relocation with no flexibility or reimbursement language may have disparate impact on caregivers, people with disabilities, or those with economic constraints.
- **Fix:** Reframe as *"Relocation assistance available for the right candidate"* or specify a reasonable timeline.

---

#### **M-5: In-Person Interview Requirement — ADA Concern**
- **Issue:** The posting states *"No phone interviews — we do this in person only."* Refusing all remote interview options may fail to accommodate candidates with mobility impairments or disabilities that make travel difficult.
- **Regulation:** ADA, 42 U.S.C. § 12112 — reasonable accommodation applies to the application and interview process, not just employment.
- **Fix:** State that alternative interview formats are available upon request as an accommodation.

---

### 🟡 LOW SEVERITY / ESCALATE FOR REVIEW

---

#### **L-1: "Unlimited PTO (but we really need people who want to work, not vacation)"**
- **Issue:** This language directly discourages use of a stated benefit and may conflict with California's prohibition on use-it-or-lose-it PTO policies. It also reinforces the overwork culture signaled elsewhere in the posting.
- **Fix:** Simply state *"Flexible/unlimited PTO policy"* without the discouraging qualifier. Consult California employment counsel on PTO policy structure.

#### **L-2: "Stock options (details to be determined)"**
- **Issue:** Advertising an undefined benefit may constitute a misrepresentation or an unenforceable promise under California law. While not a specific regulatory violation of the statutes reviewed, it creates legal exposure.
- **Fix:** Only list benefits with defined terms or add a disclaimer that details will be provided during the offer stage.
