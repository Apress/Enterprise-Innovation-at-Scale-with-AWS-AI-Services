## 2. Violations

### 🔴 HIGH SEVERITY

---

#### VIOLATION 1 — Explicit Sex/Gender Discrimination Throughout
**Regulatory Basis:** Title VII of the Civil Rights Act (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2); California FEPA

**Offending language:**
- *"She should be a natural team player"*
- *"she'll manage a team"*
- *"She will represent the company at events"*
- *"THIS IS A MALE-DOMINATED FIELD, SO WE ESPECIALLY WANT FEMALE CANDIDATES"*
- *"If you're a guy, we might consider you, but we're really looking for women for this role"*
- *"girls-friendly position"*
- *"we love to hire strong women"*
- *"the guys in sales"*

**Why it's a problem:** Title VII prohibits restricting, limiting, or classifying applicants on the basis of sex. Using gendered pronouns throughout implies only one gender is sought. Explicitly stating a gender preference — even one framed as pro-female — is facially discriminatory and unlawful unless a narrow Bona Fide Occupational Qualification (BFOQ) applies, which it plainly does not here. California's FEPA independently prohibits such conduct.

**Fix:** Use gender-neutral language throughout. Replace all gendered pronouns with *"the successful candidate"* or *"they/their."* Remove all statements expressing gender preference. Remove references to "girls," "guys," and gender-specific role framing entirely.

---

#### VIOLATION 2 — Appearance/Attractiveness as a Hiring Criterion
**Regulatory Basis:** Title VII (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2); California FEPA; Supertron LLC Corporate Hiring Standards Handbook

**Offending language:**
- *"when they're pretty enough to represent the company at client meetings"*
- *"aka: good looking"* (under "Outgoing and personable")
- *"Willingness to travel…especially when we need someone to look good at events"*
- *"Send resume, photo, and references"*

**Why it's a problem:** Requiring physical attractiveness as a job qualification is discriminatory and has no legitimate, lawful basis for a Marketing Manager role. Requesting a photo at application is a well-known EEOC red flag because it exposes race, age, gender, and disability status before any merit review, creating conditions for conscious or unconscious discriminatory screening. Supertron's own internal handbook explicitly prohibits references to "attractive appearance," "good-looking," or physical characteristics unrelated to the job.

**Fix:** Remove all appearance-based language and the photo requirement entirely. Focus qualifications on verifiable professional competencies (e.g., "demonstrated ability to represent the company professionally in client-facing settings").

---

#### VIOLATION 3 — Family Status / Caregiver Screening
**Regulatory Basis:** Title VII (sex/pregnancy discrimination); California FEPA (marital/family status); EEOC Guidance on Caregiver Discrimination

**Offending language:**
- *"No family obligations that would interfere with work"*

**Why it's a problem:** This is a direct screen against candidates with caregiving responsibilities. It disproportionately impacts women and is widely recognized by the EEOC as a form of sex-plus discrimination. California FEPA also independently prohibits discrimination based on marital and family status. There is no lawful basis for this requirement — even if overtime is genuinely required, the appropriate language is to describe the actual scheduling expectation, not to screen out people based on family structure.

**Fix:** Remove entirely. Replace with a neutral, factual description of scheduling requirements if relevant: *"This role may occasionally require extended hours during peak campaign periods."*

---

#### VIOLATION 4 — California Pay Transparency (SB 1162)
**Regulatory Basis:** California Labor Code § 244 (SB 1162), effective January 1, 2024

**Offending language:**
- *"$50,000 - $150,000 depending on what we negotiate and what you ask for"*
- *"Vague benefits package including health insurance (maybe — depends on budget)"*

**Why it's a problem:** SB 1162 requires all California employers to post a specific, good-faith pay range for any advertised position. While a range *is* present ($50K–$150K), a $100,000 spread is legally problematic because it is not a defensible good-faith range — it is effectively no range at all and may be viewed as a non-compliant placeholder. Additionally, conditioning the range on "what you ask for" reintroduces salary history dependency, which is separately banned under California law. Vague benefits language does not itself violate SB 1162 but signals a broader lack of good-faith transparency. Penalties are $100–$10,000 per violation per posting.

**Fix:** Narrow the pay range to a legitimate, role-specific band based on market data (e.g., *"$90,000 – $115,000 annually"*). Remove all language tying the range to negotiation or candidate-initiated disclosure. Provide a clear summary of benefits.

---

#### VIOLATION 5 — "Must Own a Car" Requirement
**Regulatory Basis:** EEOC disparate impact doctrine; Supertron LLC Corporate Hiring Standards Handbook

**Offending language:**
- *"Must own a car for client visits"*

**Why it's a problem:** Requiring car ownership (rather than a valid driver's license or reliable transportation) excludes lower-income candidates and those who rely on public transit, creating potential disparate impact. Supertron's own handbook specifically flags *"Must own a car"* as prohibited language when driving is not an essential, documented job function.

**Fix:** If client site visits genuinely require driving, require: *"Valid driver's license required; ability to travel to client sites as needed."* Do not require car ownership.

### 🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY

---

#### VIOLATION 6 — "Aggressive" Personality Descriptor
**Regulatory Basis:** EEOC guidance on stereotyped role language; Supertron LLC Handbook §3.4

**Offending language:**
- *"sharp, aggressive marketing manager"*

**Why it's a problem:** "Aggressive" is a gendered stereotype historically applied to men in leadership and used pejoratively against women who exhibit the same behavior. It can deter qualified female candidates from applying and has no precise, job-related meaning. Supertron's handbook flags stereotyped personality language as prohibited.

**Fix:** Replace with specific, job-relevant competencies: *"results-oriented," "decisive," "experienced in driving campaign performance."*

---

#### VIOLATION 7 — "Willing to Go Out for Drinks After Work"
**Regulatory Basis:** ADA (alcohol exclusion risk); religion (Title VII); potential sexual harassment nexus

**Offending language:**
- *"Willing to go out for drinks with the team after work"*

**Why it's a problem:** This can exclude candidates in recovery, those with religious prohibitions against alcohol, or those with caregiving responsibilities. It is not a legitimate job function and introduces liability as a de facto social screening criterion.

**Fix:** Remove entirely. If team cohesion matters, describe it neutrally: *"Collaborative team environment with regular team-building activities."*

---

#### VIOLATION 8 — Vague Qualifications ("we're not picky," "good school")
**Regulatory Basis:** EEOC best practices; Supertron LLC Handbook §C1 (Essential vs. Preferred)

**Offending language:**
- *"Marketing management experience (we're not picky about how much)"*
- *"Bachelor's degree from a good school preferred"*

**Why it's a problem:** Vague, subjective qualifications create conditions for discriminatory application of standards. "Good school" is elitist language that Supertron's own handbook flags as problematic (analogous to "Ivy League only"). Undefined experience thresholds make it impossible to evaluate candidates consistently.

**Fix:** Specify minimum requirements: *"3+ years of marketing management experience"* and *"Bachelor's degree in Marketing, Communications, or related field, or equivalent professional experience."*
